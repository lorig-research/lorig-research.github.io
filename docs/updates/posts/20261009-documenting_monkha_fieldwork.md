# Documenting Monkha in Riti and Cungseng

**09 October 2026**

Tshering Om Tamang and Sonam Lhamo, Research Assistant, continued linguistic fieldwork in Riti and Cungseng. The fieldwork focused on recording and documenting Monkha with community members, contributing to the ongoing research on the language and its use in everyday life.

They arrived in Riti, in Jigmechoeling Gewog, Sarpang Dzongkhag, on 21 September and conducted linguistic data collection from 22 to 26 September. The work involved recording language data with community members and adding to the material gathered during earlier field visits. Time spent in the village also provided opportunities to engage with speakers in their everyday surroundings and gain a better understanding of the contexts in which Monkha is used.

On 23 September, they celebrated Blessed Rainy Day with a Monpa family in Riti. Sharing the occasion offered an opportunity to experience local traditions and spend time with the family outside the formal recording sessions. The celebration was also a reminder that documenting a language involves more than recording words and conversations; it means engaging with the people, traditions and everyday experiences that give the language its place in community life.

Reaching Riti presented several challenges. A landslide caused a roadblock at Dochula, while ongoing road-widening work led to a blockage on the Trongsa–Gelephu road. In addition, the bridge leading into Riti, which had been washed away by monsoon flooding in July, had been replaced by a makeshift bridge. Although it allowed access to the village, crossing it remained precarious. These conditions added practical difficulties to the visit, but linguistic data collection continued throughout the planned period in Riti.

On 27 September, they travelled to Cungseng, where they continued collecting linguistic data until 3 October. From 5 to 7 October, Tshering collected additional linguistic data in Jigmechoeling Gewog. These visits extended the ongoing documentation of Monkha through recordings and direct engagement with speakers in the communities where the language is spoken.
